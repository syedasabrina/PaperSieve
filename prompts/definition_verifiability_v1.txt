--- WORKING DEFINITION: 3-PILLAR FRAMEWORK ---

PILLAR 1 — Expression Level (Linguistic / Author-Centric)
Subjectivity is a property of text when the author's primary intention is to communicate a personal, non-objective point of view rather than report verifiable facts. It is grounded in "private states" — internal mental or emotional experiences (opinions, evaluations, emotions, speculations, beliefs) that are not open to objective observation or verification [Quirk et al. 1985; Wiebe 1994; Banfield 1982]. Signals include affective vocabulary, evaluative framing, epistemic markers, and lexical items encoding an inner state.
Boundary conditions: Subjectivity ≠ sentiment or polarity. A sentence can be subjective without carrying polarity ("I think he went home"), and evaluative content can appear in formally objective sentences ("The earphone broke in two days"). Subjectivity at this level is graded, not binary — expressions vary in intensity and density of subjective content.

PILLAR 2 — Interpretive Level (Social / Reader-Centric)
Subjectivity is a property of judgment when no single ground truth exists because the label assigned to a text is inherently rater-dependent. Judgments vary systematically across judges depending on demographics, cultural background, religious beliefs, lived experience, or personal biases. This produces structured (not random) disagreement — different groups yield different but internally coherent labelings. This is analytically distinct from annotation error.
Includes: (a) the first-person vs. third-party distinction — self-reported and observer-inferred subjectivity are distinct and can diverge substantially; (b) "metasubjectivity" — where the very definition of a task concept (e.g., "what counts as an opinion?" or "what counts as hateful?") is itself rater- and application-dependent.

PILLAR 3 — Methodological Level (Annotation / Phenomenological)
Subjectivity is present when legitimate disagreement in human annotations constitutes meaningful signal about task ambiguity or diverse valid beliefs, rather than noise or annotator error. In these tasks, "acceptability" or "individual preference" are more valid evaluation targets than accuracy. Degree of subjectivity is measurable — operationalized as average absolute deviation, pairwise L1 distance, annotation entropy, or inverse output similarity across annotators.
Boundary condition: Disagreement alone does not confirm subjectivity. Its source must be identified. Disagreement caused by ambiguity in text, missing context, or sloppy annotation is distinct from disagreement caused by perspectival differences. Only the latter reflects Pillar 3 subjectivity.
