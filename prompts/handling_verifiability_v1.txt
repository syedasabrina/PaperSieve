--- WORKING HANDLING TAXONOMY ---

POSITION A — SUBJECTIVITY AS NOISE (Flattening / Suppression)

A1. MAJORITY VOTING / AGGREGATION
    Multiple annotators label the same instance; the majority or average
    label becomes the gold standard. Individual variation is discarded.
    Variants: simple majority, weighted majority, averaging (continuous
    scales), two-annotator adjudication with third for tie-breaking,
    latent class modeling for bias-corrected gold tag, lenient majority
    (2 of 3 must agree).

A2. PRESCRIPTIVE ANNOTATION GUIDELINES
    Disagreement treated as a failure of annotation design. Guidelines
    iterated until disagreement drops. Annotators apply a specified
    shared belief rather than personal judgment.

A3. FILTERING / EXCLUSION OF AMBIGUOUS INSTANCES
    Instances where annotators cannot agree are removed from the dataset
    rather than preserved as a distinct category.

A4. LABEL NOISE CORRECTION
    High-loss or divergent annotations treated as mislabeled instances
    and corrected algorithmically (e.g., influence functions, relabeling
    from neighboring instances).

A5. DISTANT SUPERVISION / AUTOMATIC LABELING
    Human annotation bypassed entirely; labels derived from structural
    or distributional properties of the corpus.

POSITION B — SUBJECTIVITY AS SIGNAL (Preservation / Modeling)

B1. NON-AGGREGATION (Preserving Individual Labels)
    Individual labels from all annotators retained rather than collapsed
    into a single gold standard. Full distribution of annotations is the
    target.

B2. SOFT LABEL TRAINING
    Model trained on the probability distribution of annotator labels
    rather than a single hard majority-voted label.

B3. MULTI-TASK LEARNING WITH PER-ANNOTATOR HEADS
    Each annotator modeled as a separate prediction subtask with shared
    representations and annotator-specific components.

B4. PERSONALIZED MODELING (User-Level Gold Standard)
    Each user or annotator treated as their own gold standard; models
    trained or adapted per individual (e.g., collaborative filtering,
    user-profile embeddings, Z-score normalization per annotator).

B5. CONTINUOUS SUBJECTIVITY QUANTIFICATION
    Subjectivity operationalized as a measurable, computable property
    of instances or tasks.
    Known metrics in the corpus:
      - Average Absolute Deviation: Σ|label(vi) − aj| / n
      - Pairwise L1 distance between annotator labels
      - Annotation entropy (Shannon entropy over label distribution)
      - Inverse pairwise output similarity (e.g., inverse ROUGE-L)
      - Annotation variance / standard deviation
      - Log-likelihood difference across corpus types
      - Controversy score (diversity of opinions across annotators)

B6. MODELING SUBJECTIVITY AS AUXILIARY PREDICTION TARGET
    Degree of subjectivity incorporated into model training as an
    auxiliary task alongside the primary classification objective.

B7. ANNOTATOR IDENTITY / CONTEXT AS MODEL INPUT
    Annotator demographics, sentiment, identity, or past behavior
    incorporated as a feature or embedding in the model.

B8. ANNOTATOR-CENTRIC ACTIVE LEARNING
    Data selection strategies target individual annotators to ensure
    minority perspectives are captured in training.

B9. PRESERVING DISAGREEMENT IN RELEASED DATASETS
    Datasets released with disagreement information intact rather than
    only adjudicated gold labels.
