--- WORKING TAXONOMY ---

CATEGORY A — Always Subjective
Tasks where no single ground truth exists and judges legitimately disagree due
to perspectival, demographic, or value-based differences.
Includes: sentiment analysis, opinion mining, emotion detection, hate speech
detection, toxicity detection, misogyny/sexism detection, sarcasm/irony detection,
stance detection, argument quality judgment, dialogue act classification, moral
value classification, NLG/MT quality evaluation, guilt perception prediction,
cognitive appraisal prediction, politeness/offensiveness rating, subjectivity
classification, belief identification.
Logic: Individual perception, private state expression, no recoverable ground truth.

CATEGORY B — Always Objective
Tasks where a single recoverable correct answer exists under a defined schema,
independent of annotator perspective.
Includes: POS tagging, named entity recognition, syntactic parsing, grammar
checking, factual information extraction, claim verifiability detection.
Logic: Verifiable facts or rules; annotator identity does not change the answer.

CATEGORY C — Mixed / Context-Dependent
Tasks whose subjectivity status depends on the dimension annotated, the task
framing, the annotation methodology (prescriptive vs. descriptive), or whether
subjectivity is an instance-level rather than task-level property.
Includes: summarization, machine translation, lexical complexity prediction,
reading fluency assessment, opinion expression annotation, evaluative sentence
identification, RST annotation, word sense subjectivity tagging, subjectivity
detection, forum thread classification.
Logic: Varies by intent, instance, or annotation design.

--- TASK LABEL RULES ---

SUBJECTIVE: The paper explicitly calls the task subjective, OR the paper's
framing makes clear that no single ground truth exists and annotator disagreement
is expected and legitimate.

OBJECTIVE: The paper explicitly calls the task objective, OR the paper treats
the task as having a single recoverable correct answer independent of annotator
perspective.

MIXED: The paper explicitly acknowledges that the task is partly subjective and
partly objective, OR the task involves dimensions that differ in their
subjectivity status (e.g., lower-level dimensions are objective, higher-level
dimensions are subjective).

NOT CLAIMED: The paper does not make any explicit or implicit claim about
whether the task is subjective or objective. Use this only when the paper
treats the task as a standard classification problem without any engagement
with subjectivity as a concept.

--- TAXONOMY CATEGORY RULES ---

CRITICAL — EXTRACTION TARGET DISTINCTION:
Many papers in subjectivity-related NLP use subjectivity as the phenomenon
being detected or extracted, not as a property of the annotation task itself.
For these papers, assign the taxonomy category based on HOW THE ANNOTATION
WAS TREATED, not on what the paper is trying to detect.

  - If a paper builds a classifier to detect subjective expressions but treats
    its own annotation as having a single gold standard (majority vote,
    adjudication, no disagreement modeling), classify the annotation task
    according to the taxonomy — do NOT automatically assign Category A just
    because subjectivity is the extraction target.

  - If a paper annotates subjective content and ALSO engages with annotation
    disagreement as a methodological concern (preserving labels, modeling
    divergence, questioning gold standards), then the task may be Category A
    or Category C.

MIXED CATEGORY ASSIGNMENT:
Assign Category C when:
  - The paper explicitly states the task is partly subjective and partly
    objective (e.g., different dimensions have different ground truth status).
  - The task's subjectivity depends on instance-level properties rather than
    being uniform across all instances.
  - The paper frames subjectivity as a spectrum or continuum rather than
    a binary property.
  - The paper uses both prescriptive and descriptive annotation paradigms
    for different conditions.

Do NOT assign Category A when the paper merely acknowledges annotation
difficulty without claiming diverse valid beliefs or legitimate disagreement.

NOT CLAIMED ASSIGNMENT:
Assign Not Claimed only when the paper makes no claim — direct or indirect —
about whether the task is subjective or objective. If the paper's methodology
implies a position (e.g., using personalized models because disagreement
reflects individual variation), infer the label from context rather than
defaulting to Not Claimed.

--- REASONING CODES ---

R1 — Private State Grounding: Task targets internal mental/emotional states not
     open to objective verification.
R2 — No Ground Truth: No single correct label exists; multiple valid answers
     are possible.
R3 — Annotator Identity Dependence: Labels vary systematically with annotator
     demographics, beliefs, or lived experience.
R4 — Annotation Divergence as Signal: Disagreement reflects genuine task
     ambiguity or diverse valid beliefs, not error.
R5 — Metasubjectivity: The concept definition itself (e.g., "what counts as
     hateful") varies by application context and reader.
R6 — Prescriptive Suppression: Task rendered objective by annotation design
     even though underlying content is subjective.
R7 — Instance-Level Variability: Subjectivity is a property of specific data
     points within the task, not of the task as a whole.
